## What problem this solves

Receipts and IDs can now be faked in ways the current checks do not catch:

- a payment screenshot **regenerated by an image model**, or edited with an inpainting tool, with no
  editor metadata left in it;
- a **fully AI-made** receipt, ID card or passport;
- a **face-swapped or AI-generated selfie**, or an AI "clone" of a real person's face;
- a **photo of a screen or a print** of someone else's selfie or ID;
- a genuine receipt **re-photographed, cropped or re-rendered** and sent for a second invoice, which
  an exact hash and a perceptual hash both miss;
- text that was **written by a language model** rather than typed by the payer.

The existing checks (error-level analysis, noise consistency, metadata markers, exact and perceptual
hashes, a screen-replay heuristic) are hand-written and were only ever tuned on synthetic images. They are
kept. The detectors add trained models that learn the patterns from data, on our own machines.

## Principles (these are requirements, not goals)

1. **A detector never approves and never rejects.** A high score can only send a claim to a person. A
   clean score never lifts a hold, never raises a claim's tier and never makes auto-approval easier.
2. **AI reads, rules and people decide.** The score is one more signal in the same risk score, with a
   weight an organisation can change or set to 0.
3. **Fail closed to a person, fail open on the rules.** If a model is missing, unreadable, too slow or
   throws, the detector is recorded as `skipped` with the reason and the existing rules run unchanged. A
   detector outage must never block or approve a claim by itself.
4. **No outside AI.** Training and inference both run on the customer's own server or laptop. No hosted
   model, no API key, no image or embedding leaves the machine. Production refuses to start with a
   detector setting that points at a URL (see `docs/models.md`).
5. **Every score is reproducible.** The model file's SHA-256, its version and the score are stored
   with the decision, so a past decision can be explained after the model has been replaced.
6. **No accuracy claim without a measurement.** Numbers are only quoted with the test set they came from.
   Results on synthetic fakes are labelled as such and are never presented as real-world accuracy.
7. **The payer is never accused.** The payer sees the same neutral "needs a manual check" message as
   today. Staff see a plain-language reason and, where possible, where the model looked.
8. **Shadow first.** A detector starts in `shadow` mode, records scores and changes nothing. It may move
   to `escalate` only after the acceptance gates below are met and a named person signs off.

## The detectors

All share one interface (`app/detect/base.py`, planned): an input (an image, or text), a result with a
score from 0 to 1, a label, the model version and SHA-256, optional signals and a `skipped` reason.

| Detector | Looks at | Catches | Reason code when it escalates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Receipt / PoP** | Payment screenshots and PDFs (rendered to an image) | Edited, pasted, inpainted or fully AI-generated proofs | `FORGED_POP_SUSPECTED`, `AI_GENERATED_SUSPECTED` |
| **ID document** | ID card and passport photos (front) | Forged or AI-generated documents | `AI_GENERATED_SUSPECTED`, plus the existing `DOC_TAMPER_SUSPECTED` family |
| **Face** | The aligned face crop of the selfie and the ID portrait | Deepfake or face-swapped selfie, AI-generated or cloned face, screen or print replay | `DEEPFAKE_SUSPECTED`, `SYNTHETIC_FACE_SUSPECTED` |
| **Near-duplicate** | An image embedding of every stored proof | A receipt that was re-photographed, cropped, recompressed or re-rendered | `NEAR_DUPLICATE_EMBEDDING` (joins the duplicate family, so the claim goes to `DUPLICATE_HOLD`) |
| **Text** (optional) | OCR or pasted text | Text written by a language model | `AI_TEXT_SUSPECTED` (advisory only) |

A model that cannot decide reports `DETECTOR_UNCERTAIN` (see *Calibration and abstention*). That is shown
to the reviewer and never counted as a fake.

## How the models are built (machine learning and deep learning, all local)

The design assumes **few confirmed fakes and many genuine documents**, which is the real situation of a
new deployment. Each technique below has a reason; none needs a hosted service.

- **Supervised head plus a one-class model.** A supervised classifier is trained on synthetic fakes and
  on reviewer-confirmed fakes. Alongside it, a one-class model is trained on genuine documents only (a small
  convolutional autoencoder's reconstruction error, and the distance of the image embedding from the
  genuine cluster). Forgery types nobody has labelled yet still look unlike genuine.
- **Self-supervised pretraining on our own data.** The backbone first learns from unlabelled genuine
  receipts, IDs and selfies with contrastive learning (SimCLR or BYOL style) using realistic augmentations:
  crop, blur, JPEG, screen capture, colour change. This gives a domain-specific backbone with no labels
  and no outside model.
- **Forensic input channels (built).** A manifest names the planes a model reads, in order: `rgb` (3), `noise` (the residual, 1), `fft`
  (the spectrum, 1) and `ela` (error-level analysis, 1), so `["rgb","noise","fft","ela"]` is a 6-channel input. They are
  deterministic recipes in `app/detect/features.py`, so training and serving prepare the picture identically. The network sees more than RGB: a high-pass **noise residual** (fixed
  SRM-style filters), the **frequency spectrum** (FFT or DCT, where generators and upscalers leave
  periodic artefacts) and the existing ELA map. These transfer across generators better than pixels alone.
- **Metric learning for duplicates.** The embedding is trained with a contrastive or triplet loss: the same
  receipt photographed, cropped or re-rendered is a positive; different receipts, **including the same
  template with a different amount**, are hard negatives. Search is cosine similarity; the match threshold is
  set at a target false-match rate, not guessed.
- **Faces.** A lightweight CNN on the aligned crop with the same noise and frequency channels, plus
  consistency checks the system already has (selfie-to-ID embedding drift from the face matcher). Models are
  evaluated per group before they may escalate (see *Fairness*).
- **Calibration and abstention.** Raw scores are calibrated (temperature scaling or isotonic regression) on
  a held-out set so a score means a probability. **Conformal prediction** lets the model say "uncertain"
  instead of guessing; uncertain goes to a person like any other hold. `kyc/calibration.py` already supplies
  AUC, bootstrap intervals and threshold-at-a-target-rate.
- **Ensemble and test-time augmentation.** A few augmented views are averaged and combined with the rule
  signals by a small logistic stacker. The rules remain the fallback when a model is absent.
- **Hard-negative mining and active learning.** Reviewer outcomes are the most valuable labels. Training
  picks the most uncertain and most disputed cases first (consent-gated; see `training-data.md`).
- **Continual learning without forgetting.** A retrain uses new labels plus a replay set of old cases. Each
  retrain is a new, versioned model, compared with the current one on a **frozen benchmark**, and promoted only
  if it is better and no per-group gap widens. Rolling back is putting the previous file back.
- **Compression for laptops.** A larger "teacher" trained on a GPU box can be distilled into a small
  "student"; models are exported to ONNX and int8-quantised. The accuracy lost to compression is measured and
  written into the model card. Targets: each detector at most about 25 MB and about 150 ms per image on four
  CPU cores (measured, not assumed).
- **Drift and robustness monitoring, offline.** Input drift (embedding mean and covariance against the
  training set), score-distribution shift and a periodic self-test: generate fresh synthetic fakes with the
  current generators and check they are still flagged. Alerts go through the existing ops outbox.
- **Adversarial robustness.** Training augmentations imitate evasion: re-compression, a screenshot of a
  screenshot, added noise, resizing. A separate evaluation split is built only from those. This is a known
  weakness and is documented as one, never as a guarantee.
- **Explainability.** A Grad-CAM style heatmap is stored next to the ELA heatmap so a reviewer can see where the
  model looked.

## What the engine does with a score

1. After the existing tamper checks, each enabled detector scores the file and the result is stored in
   `detector_scores` (append-only) with the model version and SHA-256, and in the run's `checks`.
2. In **`shadow`** mode that is all. Nothing else reads the score.
3. In **`escalate`** mode a score at or above the organisation's threshold for that detector adds the reason
   code above and a risk signal. The claim then goes to `PENDING_REVIEW` (or `DUPLICATE_HOLD` for a near-duplicate)
   and **cannot be auto-approved** while the signal stands. A score below the threshold changes nothing.
4. Reviewers see an **Authenticity** card: the score, the label, the model version, a "shadow" or "live" badge,
   a plain-language reason and a heatmap where available.
5. The reason codes a detector adds are **staff-only**: the payer-facing claim view hides them (`INTERNAL_REASONS`), like `SUSPECTED_FAKE`.
6. Resolved alerts (`confirmed_fake`, `false_positive`) are compared with the shadow scores in the scorecard,
   so the real precision and recall of each detector is visible before anyone switches it on.

Signal weights are starting points to be set from measured precision (they are policy, per organisation):

| Signal | Proposed default weight | Dampened when the money is confirmed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `NEAR_DUPLICATE_EMBEDDING` | counts as `REUSED_RECEIPT` (70) | no (reuse is never dampened) |
| `AI_GENERATED_SUSPECTED` | 55 | no (like `AI_GENERATED_MARKERS`) |
| `DEEPFAKE_SUSPECTED` | none: a KYC flag (sends the verification to `NEEDS_REVIEW`) | n/a |
| `SYNTHETIC_FACE_SUSPECTED` | none: a KYC flag | n/a |
| `FORGED_POP_SUSPECTED` | 45 | yes (file-only doubt) |
| `AI_TEXT_SUSPECTED` | none yet (the optional text detector is not built) | yes |

KYC verifications have no fraud-risk score: the face and ID-document detectors add their reason codes to the integrity flags, which
send a verification to a person. Only the claim detectors carry weights. A weight of 0 still switches a signal off (the claim
still goes to a person; it just adds no score).

## Settings (planned)

| Setting | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POPS_DETECT_MODE` | `off` | `off`, `shadow` or `escalate`. A global switch; the policy can only narrow what it allows (turn kinds off, raise thresholds), never widen it. |
| `POPS_DETECT_MODEL_DIR` | `./var/models/detect` | Folder holding the model files and `manifest.json`. A path outside it, or any URL, is refused in production. |
| `POPS_DETECT_DUPLICATE_SCAN_LIMIT` | `20000` | Near-duplicate search compares a new picture with at most this many of the newest stored embeddings. Brute-force cosine search is exact and needs no extra database extension; past this size, a vector index (such as pgvector) is the next step. |
| `POPS_DETECT_DUPLICATE_DAYS` | `180` | Only embeddings from the last this-many days are compared. |
| policy `detect_enabled_kinds` | all | Which detectors run for the organisation: `receipt`, `doc`, `face`, `duplicate`, `text`. |
| policy `detect_thresholds` | unset: the threshold the model was published with in `manifest.json`; with neither, the detector never escalates | Score at or above which a detector escalates. Set from the measured false-flag rate. |

The near-duplicate model's output is an embedding vector (the others output one probability); it is stored as float16 bytes of an
L2-normalised vector with the model's SHA-256, vectors from different model files are never compared, and in `escalate` mode a
score (the best cosine similarity) at or above the threshold holds **both** claims, like the other duplicate checks. The
search is across organisations, like the hash registry; no other organisation's data is shown. Retention deletes the embedding
with its file.

The model folder holds one file per detector and a `manifest.json` with each file's name, version, SHA-256,
input specification, calibration, thresholds and a pointer to its model card. A file whose hash does not match
the manifest is not loaded (recorded as `skipped: hash_mismatch`).

## Acceptance gates before `escalate` (an operator signs these off)

A detector may leave `shadow` only when its model card shows, on a **held-out set that includes real
documents from this deployment**:

1. the lower bound of the AUC's bootstrap confidence interval is above the target agreed with the
   organisation;
2. the false-flag rate on genuine documents at the chosen threshold is at or below the agreed rate (a
   reviewer's time is the cost of a false flag);
3. for face detectors, the false-flag rates by group (skin tone, age, lighting, phone class) differ by no more
   than the agreed gap, and every group has enough samples to measure it (the calibration tool says when it
   does not);
4. results on the evasion split are reported, and the shadow period has run long enough to compare scores with
   reviewer outcomes;
5. the model card is filled in and signed (template: `docs/model-cards/TEMPLATE.md`).

If any gate cannot be met, the detector stays in shadow. Switching `POPS_DETECT_MODE` to `off` is the rollback.

## Fairness and privacy

- **Face models can be unfair.** Detectors trained mostly on lighter-skinned faces misflag darker-skinned ones.
  Per-group evaluation is a gate, and the model card lists the groups tested and their counts. Where a group
  cannot be measured, the card says so and the detector does not escalate on faces.
- **Embeddings and face crops are personal data.** They are covered by the retention policy, the payer
  consent notice, the audit trail and the private-deployment rules. Evidence purged under retention has its
  embeddings and detector heatmaps purged with it.
- **Training data stays on the box.** Datasets and weights live under `./var/` and are never uploaded.

## What it cannot do

- It does not prove a document is genuine. A clean score is **absence of evidence**, not proof. The money
  still has to match a credit on our side.
- AI-generated-content detection **degrades against generators it has not seen**. Treat it as advisory and
  re-measure when new generators appear; the periodic self-test exists to notice this.
- A determined attacker can adapt (recompress, rephotograph, add noise). The evasion split measures this; it
  cannot remove it.
- Models trained only on synthetic fakes learn the synthetic generators. Their numbers do not describe real
  fraud until reviewer-confirmed fakes from this deployment have been added.
- Text detection is the least reliable of the five. It is optional and only ever adds a small advisory weight.

## Operations

- Models live in `POPS_DETECT_MODEL_DIR`, mounted read-only, like the other models.
- `GET /admin/detectors` (admin) shows each detector's mode, version, SHA-256 and whether it loaded.
- Every run records `model_versions["detectors"]`; the audit packet shows them.
- Training and evaluation run offline on a laptop (CPU, small data, resumable) or a private GPU server; see
  `docs/training-data.md`.
- Running the retrain and the self-test on a schedule is the operator's job; results are written to the model
  card and flagged through the ops outbox when a detector's measured quality drops.
